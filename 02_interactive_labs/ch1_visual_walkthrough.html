AI, Machine Learning,
and Deep Learning
— they're not the same thing

🧠

Artificial Intelligence

Any technique that makes a computer do something that normally requires human intelligence — from chess engines to spell-checkers to voice assistants.

📊

Machine Learning

A subset of AI where the computer learns rules from data instead of having rules written by hand. You show it 1,000 emails marked spam/not-spam — it figures out the pattern.

⚡

Deep Learning

A subset of ML using neural networks — layers of math inspired by the brain. This is what powers LLMs like ChatGPT, image recognition, speech-to-text, and everything in this course.

💡 One-sentence version: AI is the goal, machine learning is the method, deep learning is the most powerful tool for that method. Every chapter in this course lives inside the innermost circle.

Before we can talk about learning…

What Is a Model,
Exactly?

Think of a model as a machine with thousands of dials. Each dial (called a weight) changes how strongly the machine responds to a piece of input. At first, all the dials are set randomly. Training is simply the process of slowly turning each dial in the right direction until the machine's output is useful.

The Loss Function:
Measuring How Wrong You Are

Before the model can improve, it needs a score for how badly it's doing. That score is called loss (or error). Think of it like archery — the loss is the distance between where your arrow landed and where you were aiming.

Low loss = close to the bullseye. High loss = way off. The model's entire job is to drive this number toward zero.

Prediction: "cat" · Truth: "cat" loss = 0.02

Almost perfect — barely needed to adjust the weights. The arrow hit the bullseye.

Prediction: "dog" · Truth: "cat" loss = 2.4

Wrong, but related — both are small furry animals. The model had some idea but guessed wrong.

Prediction: "airplane" · Truth: "cat" loss = 8.4

Way off. The model is basically guessing randomly. Lots of weight adjustment needed.

Mean Squared Error — the most common loss function
Loss = (predicted − actual)²
squaring makes all errors positive and punishes big mistakes harder

Gradient Descent:
How the Model Fixes Itself

Now the model knows how wrong it is (loss). But how does it get less wrong? Gradient descent — it checks which direction makes the loss go down, then takes a small step in that direction. Repeat a million times. That's training.

Imagine you're blindfolded on a hilly landscape and want to reach the lowest valley. You feel the slope under your feet and take a step downhill. That step size is the learning rate. Too big and you overshoot; too small and it takes forever.

Putting It All Together

The Training Loop

①

Feed Data

Show the model a training example — a sentence, image, or Q&A pair.

②

Forward Pass

The current weights produce a prediction — usually wrong at first.

③

Compute Loss

Measure how wrong the prediction was — get a single number (the loss).

④

Backward Pass

Use calculus to find which direction to nudge each weight to lower the loss.

⑤

Update Weights & Repeat

Nudge every weight a tiny bit in the right direction. Then go back to step ①. Do this millions of times. That's what it means to "train" a model. Every step the loss goes down a little — and the model gets a little smarter.
